## Checklist

Prefer:

- Plain English
- Concrete nouns and verbs
- Short paragraphs
- Strong conceptual distinctions
- Observable evidence over vague claims
- Memorable terms, each supported by a definition
- Direct acknowledgment of tradeoffs

Avoid:

- Generic leadership inspiration
- Inflated AI language
- Empty transformation claims
- Corporate filler
- Excessive jargon
- Treating every friction as waste
- Treating speed as pressure on individuals
- Assuming a local optimisation is good for the whole system
- **Em dashes in finished copy**

## The two bodies of work

Both express the same worldview — *better action depends on seeing the system
more clearly* — and should never read as unrelated interests.

**Working Faster** — makes the real state of organisational work visible. Opaque
status, hidden queues, unclear ownership, feedback latency.

**Context Engineering and Knowledge Systems** — makes meaning, evidence,
constraints, and relationships visible to software and AI. Ontology, context
before action, retrieval and evidence, agent architecture.

## Two failure modes

**Forcing everything into one sentence.** The umbrella idea connects the work; it
does not have to appear in every hero, headline, or bio. Introduce the point of
view, then let people choose a path.

**More biography instead of more proof.** The differentiator is not that Jeff has
opinions about work. It is that he models problems precisely enough to produce
usable tools. Reach for an artifact, diagram, or case study before reaching for
another paragraph of background.
