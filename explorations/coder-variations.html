Trade-offs

  • A (thin solid) — Safest. Same DNA, less weight. No new design language. Silk-screens perfectly.
  • B (subtle pen) — The swell gives warmth without going full calligraphy. Each piece still reads as geometry. The ellipse dot feels more natural than a circle.
  • C (dramatic ink) — Most expressive. The teardrop dot and whip-thin hook tail give real pen energy. Risk: might feel too organic against the geometric background rect.
  • D (bold mono) — The squared dot and pipe stem say "typed" immediately. Rounded-rect dot vs circle changes the character. Lightest conceptual change — it's still tectonic, just with mono DNA.
  • E (thin pen) — Lightest version with personality. The taper is subtle enough to feel intentional, not accidental. Best for contexts where the icon is small.
  • F (mono + ink) — Hybrid: recognizable character shapes that swell like pen strokes. "Hand-written code." The square dot grounds it in mono; the swelling stem and thinning hook tail add humanity.
