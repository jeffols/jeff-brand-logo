Rotational depth stack

Eight copies of the tectonic j, each rotated one step further back, opacity ramping from near-invisible at the rear to solid at the front. The front copy stays upright at full opacity so the mark itself never stops being the mark — everything behind it is echo. Step angle is the whole decision

Every other parameter tunes; this one changes what the mark is. Same eight layers, same opacity ramp, same recession — only the angle between them differs.

What each band buys you

Trade-offs, not a recommendation. The favicon column is the constraint that actually bites: at 16px everything above roughly 10° turns into a smudge with no j in it.

2–5°
Ghosting. Reads as a printing misregistration or a slight blur. Survives every size intact because it barely changes the silhouette — which is also the argument against it: at favicon scale it is indistinguishable from the mark you already have.
6–10°
Stacked plates. Discrete layers stay individually visible, so it reads as cards fanned out rather than motion blur. Closest to the tectonic idea already in the mark — offset rigid pieces, not a smear. Still collapses gracefully to the plain j at 32px.
12–20°
Comet. The dot detaches into a bead trail arcing over the stem and the hook throws a wide fan. Genuinely striking at 512px and up, and it is the point where the composition starts competing with the letter instead of supporting it. Dead at favicon size.
22–45°
Mandala. At 45° the eight layers close a full circle and the j dissolves into a pinwheel. Beautiful as a standalone graphic — a section divider, a loading state, a large format print. Not a logo any more.
