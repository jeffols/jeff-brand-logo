Making the fade actually visible

You were right that the fade was hard to read. A layer at alpha a composites to bg + a·(glyph − bg), so on a dark plate with a bright glyph anything under about 0.15 lands in a muddy near-background olive the eye files as noise. Your falloff of 3.0 put the rear plates at 0.08 and 0.11 — a 3% gap, invisible. The floor matters more than the curve. Recession stays at 0.06 throughout; step 6° and 9° are both here.

What each ramp costs you

whisper
Your current setting. Breaks at 4 layers — 0.08 → 0.11 is not a step, so you pay for a plate you cannot see. Fine at 3, where the single echo at 0.20 is at least distinct from the ground.
soft
The smallest lift that fixes it. Every plate clears the background, and the stack still reads as one mark with shadows rather than as three marks.
even
Linear in alpha. Mathematically tidy, but perceived lightness is roughly a power curve, so the front steps look smaller than the rear ones — the top of the ladder compresses.
graded
Linear to the eye: alpha steps widen toward the front (0.26 → 0.41 → 0.67 → 1.0) to cancel that compression. The most legible four-plate ladder here, and the one I'd defend if you want the layer count to read as a count.
strong
The mirror of whisper's failure, at the other end: 0.83 against 1.00 is indistinguishable, so the front two plates merge into one mass. Heavy and graphic, but you lose a plate again.
Compose them: --preset plates-wide --layers 4 --fade graded
Ramps override rear opacity and falloff only; geometry comes from the preset. Explicit flags beat both.
